How to use Airora

  1. 1

    Choose your country

    Tap the flag at the top. Airora guesses your country the first time; search any of 240 countries or pick Whole world for the most-loved stations everywhere.

  2. 2

    Browse or search

    Stations are sorted by how many people listen. Use the genre chips — News, Pop, Bollywood, Telugu, Classical, Sports — or type a station name or city in the search box.

  3. 3

    Tap to listen

    Tap any station and it starts in a second. The bar at the bottom keeps playing while you browse; tap it to open the full player with the live visualizer.

  4. 4

    Make it yours

    Tap ♥ to save favourites, use ◀ ▶ to hop between stations, set a sleep timer, share a station link, and keep listening with the screen off.

Questions

Is Airora really free?

Yes. No ads, no subscription and no account. It's open source under the MIT License.

Where do the stations come from?

From Radio Browser, a free community directory of internet radio that checks every station daily. Airora shows only stations that passed the latest check, most-listened first.

A station doesn't play. Why?

Stations go offline sometimes, and Airora will tell you and let you try again or skip. Some older stations only broadcast on plain “http” links, which web browsers block on secure sites — those are marked APP and play in the Android, Windows and Mac apps.

Does it keep playing with the screen off?

Yes, in the Android app (with controls in the notification) and in the desktop apps. On iPhone, add it to your Home Screen for the best experience.

How much data does it use?

Most stations stream at 64–128 kbps: roughly 30–60 MB per hour. The quality is shown on every station.

Do you collect my data?

No. Favourites and recents stay on your device. When you play a station, Airora tells Radio Browser it was played (like a public “play count”) — nothing about you.
